Federal Budget Browser

A Tool for Navigating Federal Spending

"A billion here, a billion there, and pretty soon you're talking about real money."
U.S. Senator Everett Dirkson.

Each year the federal government spends trillions of dollars. This Federal Budget Browser can help you navigate how those dollars are spent. The Browser is simple to operate — you can probably figure it out with just a little playing. Here is a summary of its features:

Main Display
Items are displayed in a table with spending amount in one column and item name in the other. Click on a column heading to sort display accordingly. If an item name is a link, click on it for more detail.
Display By
Selects category to display. Number of items in category is indicated in parentheses. Caution: a category with a large number of items may be slow to display.
Narrow Results To
When you click on an item-name link in the main display, the next screen shows that item and its category as an entry in the "Narrow Results To" section. The main display is restricted to categories and items common to entries in this section. To remove an entry from this section, click on its item name.
This Implicitly Narrows Results To
If a category only has one item that applies to the "Narrow Results To" section, that category and item are listed in the "Implicitly Narrows Results To" section. Clicking on the the item moves it to the "Narrows Results To" section; doing so has no effect on the main display.
Start Over
Resets browser to default display.
Change Year
Select a fiscal year to browse.
Normalization
Use this field to scale federal spending to any desired amount. If left blank, items are displayed as actual dollars spent. If a numerical amount is entered, total federal spending for the year is set to that amount, and individual spending items are scaled in proportion. The displayed precision matches that of the entered value; for example, if 123456.000 is entered, then items are displayed to three decimal places. Normalization is sometimes helpful in making sense of the huge numbers involved in federal spending. For example, if normalization of 100.00 is entered, spending items are displayed as percentages of total spending. Or if normalization is set to the amount of taxes you paid for the year, then you can see directly how your tax dollars were spent.

The Browser uses the Outlays dataset of the Public Budget Database for fiscal year 2022, published by the White House Office of Management and Budget. The Browser covers fiscal years from 1982 to 2026. For fiscal years 2021 to 2026, inclusive, it uses projected spending. For fiscal years 1982 to 2020, inclusive, it uses actual spending.

The name "Federal Budget Browser" is a bit imprecise since off-budget spending (such as Social Security) is included. This kind of imprecision occurs frequently in the literature. For example, the Public Budget Database includes off-budget spending as well.
